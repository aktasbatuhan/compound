Cost estimate

compound estimate prices a workload on other models and hosts without making a single call. Give it your logs, or four numbers, and it tells you what you pay today and what each alternative would cost a month, as a range.

# from logs (see Workload shape for the formats it reads)
compound estimate traces/ --current openai/gpt-6-sol \
    --candidates deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash,z-ai/glm-5.3 --monthly-calls 1000000

# from a few numbers
compound estimate --current openai/gpt-6-sol --candidates z-ai/glm-5.3 \
    --monthly-calls 1000000 --input-tokens 3500 --output-tokens 1000 \
    --cached-share 0.2 --reuse-share 0.6 --turkish-share 0.7

Each row is one place to buy a model: the model's own listing, a pinned host, or a batch tier. Rows say which lever they pull. Same model, other host and batch tier keep the model you use today. Switch model rows are labelled "quality not yet measured", because a cheaper model is only cheaper if it is good enough, and that takes an eval on your own traffic.

How a call is priced

Every call is priced on its own, with the prices that apply to its size and its time. Some listings charge more above a prompt size, and some hosts charge less at certain UTC hours or on weekends. Both are applied per call, so traffic that runs at night is priced as night traffic.

A call costs its uncached input at the input price, its cached input at the cache price, and its output at the output price. A host that lists no cache price gets no cache discount.

Why the answer is a range

Two things are uncertain until we measure them on your traffic, and the range shows both.

Caching. The cheap end assumes the host caches as much as your prompts allow, which is the "cache could serve" share from the workload profile. The expensive end assumes only what your current host already serves.

Tokens. The same text is a different number of tokens on different models, and Turkish varies far more than English. On the same Turkish text, DeepSeek V4's tokenizer produces about 399 tokens per 1,000 characters, against 256 to 316 for Mistral Large, Gemma, gpt-oss, Qwen and GLM, while English sits between 184 and 190 for all of them. The estimate moves your token counts with these measured ratios. Claude and Gemini do not publish their tokenizers, so for them the range covers every tokenizer we measured instead of assuming a match. OpenAI models are assumed to use o200k_base.

The ratios come from scripts/measure_tokenizers.py, which records the exact Wikipedia article revisions it used, so the numbers can be checked and redone.

What it does not model yet

The result lists these every time:

  • how much a different model reasons on your workload; output, including reasoning, is assumed to scale with the tokenizer ratio
  • cache write fees
  • retries and failed calls
  • whether a cheaper model is good enough

Prices

Prices come from OpenRouter's public listings. compound prices saves a dated snapshot, with per-host prices for the models you name, and --catalog pins an estimate to it, so the same inputs give the same answer later:

compound prices --hosts openai/gpt-6-sol,z-ai/glm-5.3 --out prices.json
compound estimate ... --catalog prices.json

Without --catalog, the estimate fetches current prices. A snapshot from 31 October 2026 is kept in benchmarks/prices/.
